Win test management deals with Test Manager

A field guide for BDRs, AEs and SEs: who to target, how to qualify, and how to win against TestRail, Zephyr, Xray, qTest and spreadsheets. The customer-facing story for Stage 3 lives in the Test Manager presentation. Switch the account type above and the scripts change with it.

Overview

How to use this play

You are viewing the existing customer play: for accounts that already buy from TestMu but do not use Test Manager fully. It is an attach motion with the account manager involved from the first step. Stages 1 and 2 change; the problem framing, alternatives and objections still apply.

The buyer already keeps test cases somewhere: TestRail, Zephyr, Xray, qTest or a spreadsheet. Their cases, their runs and their automation results usually sit in different places. Test Manager brings them together, drafts test cases with AI from tickets and documents, and connects to execution on the TestMu cloud. Do not sell it as a cheaper repository. Sell it as the place where planning, authoring and results meet.

Where to focus

Best fit

Teams on TestRail, Xray Cloud, Zephyr Scale or qTest, with a mix of manual and automated tests and a Jira or Azure DevOps workflow.

Fastest path

Existing TestMu customers first: their automation already runs with us. Then competitor users with a renewal 90+ days out, then teams on spreadsheets.

Best attach

Accounts evaluating KaneAI. Test Manager is the system of record behind KaneAI and Kane CLI, so the two sell together.

Do not say

  • That we are cheaper than TestRail. At list, Premium is $49 a seat and TestRail Professional is about $38 to $39.
  • "No manual rework", or that everything migrates. Run and execution history stay behind on the documented paths. Say: "Your test assets move. Keep the old tool for audit history."
  • That we support on-prem. Test Manager is cloud only.
  • That one-click works for Xray or Zephyr on Data Center, or Zephyr Enterprise. It does not.
  • "Up to 60% less creation time". We have no customer source for it.
  • Boomi or Transavia as Test Manager proof. Those are HyperExecute execution numbers.
  • A price match or a discount. Check commercial terms with your team first.

Six reasons test management breaks down

Results in two places

Manual results in one tool, automated results in CI or another dashboard.

Writing cases takes too long

Requirements change faster than testers can write and update cases.

A repository nobody trusts

Duplicates and stale cases pile up, so people write new ones.

Weak traceability

It is hard to go from a ticket to its tests to their latest results.

Readiness by spreadsheet

Someone assembles the release report by hand before every go or no-go.

Tools that stop at planning

The test plan lives in one tool; running it needs CI, runners and devices from somewhere else.

The costHours on test adminCoverage nobody can proveSlower release decisionsMore tools to pay for

Test Manager vs the alternatives · technical view

vs TestRail

One platform, not a cheaper repository

  • Execution. TestRail records results from your own CI. We can also run tests on our browsers and devices (separate plan).
  • Version history. On Premium. TestRail keeps versioning with approvals to Enterprise.
  • Duplicates. AI scan with a match score and side-by-side review.
  • Authoring. AI generation with up to 2,000 credits per seat per month on Premium, and a direct path to KaneAI.

TestRail wins on entry price, storage (50 GB and up against our 10 GB per user) and self-hosting. Do not argue these. Qualify them early.

vs Xray and Zephyr

Beyond the Jira project

  • Automation. Manual, automated and KaneAI results in one view.
  • Execution. A manual instance launches straight onto real browsers and devices.
  • Not only Jira. Azure DevOps and Linear too.
  • Still in the ticket. Marketplace apps show linked cases and execution history in Jira.

Teams that want everything inside Jira may value that more. On Data Center there is no one-click path, so raise it before the demo.

vs spreadsheets

Move when reporting starts to hurt

  • Import. CSV keeps steps, custom fields and folders.
  • History. Versions and audit logs a sheet cannot give.
  • Reporting. Run status and trends without building them by hand.

The Free plan has no imports, custom fields, issue linking or reports. A real team will need Premium, so say so early.

vs BrowserStack Test Management

Move it with the rest of the estate

  • Consolidation. Sell it inside a wider move of browser, device and test management.

There is no one-click path from BrowserStack in the docs, so plan a CSV route with the SE.
