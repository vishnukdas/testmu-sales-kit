Win performance testing deals with HyperExecute

A field guide for BDRs, AEs and SEs: who to target, how to qualify, and how to win against self-hosted load rigs and BlazeMeter. The technical story for Stage 3 lives in the performance testing presentation. Switch the account type above and the scripts change with it.

Overview

How to use this play

You are viewing the existing customer play: for accounts that already buy from TestMu, whether or not they use HyperExecute today. It is an expansion motion with the account manager involved from the first step. Stages 1 and 2 change; the problem framing, alternatives and objections still apply.

The buyer already has load scripts. They run them on servers they maintain, or pay a separate tool like BlazeMeter. HyperExecute runs the same JMeter, Gatling and k6 scripts with load split across multiple regions in one job, creates the load generators for each run and removes them after, and sits on the same platform as their functional tests.

Target market: enterprise companies in India and the Americas whose customer-facing apps face traffic peaks.

Coach's noteBDRs own Stage 1 and hand over a qualified meeting. AEs pick up with discovery, the problem framing, the HyperExecute mapping, the "JMeter is free" comparison and the business case. SEs join Stage 2 when the buyer is technical and run Stage 3.

Where to focus

Industries

E-commerce and retail, BFSI (banking, insurance, payments, broking), travel and ticketing, media, streaming and gaming, public digital platforms, B2B SaaS.

Regions

India and the Americas. Load can come from multiple regions across the Americas and Asia in the same job.

Fastest path

Existing HyperExecute customers first, then TestMu customers on other products, then new logos with a BlazeMeter or self-hosted JMeter setup.

Do not say

  • That Locust or LoadRunner scripts run as they are. They do not.
  • A price. Pricing comes from deal desk. The report screens in the presentation are illustrative.
Stage 3 · SE

Show it on the story page, then prove it on their own plan.

The demo uses the performance testing presentation. The POC runs one of their real scripts against their own environment and compares it with the numbers they have today.

Goal

A parity run that meets the success criteria agreed in Stage 2.

Before the POC
  • Their .jmx, Gatling or k6 script and data files
  • Target environment URL and allowlisting sorted
  • Last baseline: p90, throughput, error rate
Hand back to the AE
  • Readout with their numbers next to ours
  • Plan size from the Load lab

Demo path

For a new logo, lead with the problem and global load. Open the presentation in its New to HyperExecute view.

For a HyperExecute customer, lead with "same account" and CI, and open the presentation in its Already a customer view. For a TestMu customer on other products, open the New to HyperExecute view and lead with "same TestMu account".

POC plan

No new account or credentials: the POC runs under the TestMu account they already have. If they are not on HyperExecute yet, ask the account team to enable it for the POC, and check whether security wants to review HyperExecute specifically. Include the AM in the readout.

Day 0
Scope

Pick one to three plans for the journeys that matter most. Agree target users, duration, regions and pass criteria in writing.

Day 1 to 2
Dry run

Upload the plan and run it at 5 to 10 percent of target load in one region. Plugin or sampler failures show up here.

Week 1
Parity run

Run at target load with the Total Load Distribution override on. Compare p90, throughput and error rate with their last baseline for the same build.

Week 2
Make it routine

Gatling and k6: add hyperexecute.yaml and the CLI step to their pipeline. JMeter: schedule nightly runs with Workflows.

Readout
Hand back to the AE

Their baseline next to the parity run, what moved over cleanly, what still needs work, and the plan size.
