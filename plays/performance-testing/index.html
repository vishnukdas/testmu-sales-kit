Win performance testing deals with HyperExecute

A field guide for BDRs, AEs and SEs: who to target, how to qualify, and how to win against self-hosted load rigs and BlazeMeter. The technical story for Stage 3 lives in the performance testing presentation. Switch the account type above and the scripts change with it.

Overview

How to use this play

You are viewing the existing customer play: for accounts that already buy from TestMu, whether or not they use HyperExecute today. It is an expansion motion with the account manager involved from the first step. Stages 1 and 2 change; the problem framing, alternatives and objections still apply.

The buyer already has load scripts. They run them on servers they maintain, or pay a separate tool like BlazeMeter. HyperExecute runs the same JMeter, Gatling and k6 scripts with load split across multiple regions in one job, creates the load generators for each run and removes them after, and sits on the same platform as their functional tests.

Six reasons load testing breaks down

Peak pressure

Sales, launches and deadlines bring traffic the app has never seen, with little time to rehearse.

Load rig upkeep

Generators to build, patch, scale and pay for, even when no test is running.

One location

Load from a single place misses the latency, CDN and network paths users in other markets get.

Late results

Reports are pieced together after the run, so the release waits or ships without them.

Untrusted results

When a test fails, nobody can say whether the app or the load rig gave out first.

Two of everything

A separate tool, contract, credentials and security review for load, next to functional testing.

HyperExecute vs the alternatives · technical view

vs self-hosted JMeter, Gatling or k6

Why the open-source rig alone falls short

  • Servers between tests. Someone builds, patches and pays for the load rig even when nothing runs.
  • Scale is a ticket. Doubling the users means requesting and configuring more machines.
  • One location. Load from several regions means a rig in each region.
  • Reports by hand. Results are pieced together after the run.
  • App or rig? No easy view of generator health when a test fails.

Do not run down JMeter, Gatling or k6. They are strong tools and the customer keeps their scripts. Win on running them.

vs BlazeMeter

Same scripts, one platform

  • Consolidation. Functional and load testing on one contract, one set of credentials, one security review.
  • Existing customers. Load testing under the TestMu account they already use.
  • No rewrite. .jmx plans and CSV data move over as they are.
  • Renewal timing. A parity run before the BlazeMeter renewal date.

Do not pitch "no servers to manage" against BlazeMeter: it is cloud-hosted too. BlazeMeter is stronger on Locust, Private Locations and built-in pass/fail criteria, so qualify those out early.
